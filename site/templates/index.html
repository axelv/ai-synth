An experiment

Describe a sound. Get a synth you can play.

Not a sample and not a recording. What comes back is a patch: a real synth with knobs on it, running in your browser. Press play to hear one, then take the keys yourself.

Ask for a sound
__HERO_NAME__, one of __COUNT__ instruments below. Open it on its own page, which is where a MIDI keyboard works.

Getting started is three clicks

  1. Press play. It plays itself a phrase. Nothing to install, no account, no plugin, and no audio leaves your machine: the whole synth is in the page.
  2. Take the keys. Touch them, or play your computer keyboard: z x c for the lower octave, q w e for the upper. Plug a MIDI keyboard into an instrument's own page and it plays that instead.
  3. Turn the knobs. Four to seven of them, named for what a musician would call them rather than for the parameter underneath. It is a patch, so it keeps dialling long after the first sound.

__COUNT__ instruments

One page each, and each page is one file you can send to somebody. Nothing is fetched while it runs.

__CARDS__

Ask for a sound

Tell me what you want to hear, in your own words. Warm 80s pad. The bass on that track. Something glassy that sits under a vocal. Or paste a link to a clip.

I build it by hand and send back a link you can play, usually within a day. There is no queue and no signup, because there is no service yet: this page exists to find out whether one is worth building.

__REQUEST__

Or build one yourself

Nothing above was hand-written. A Claude Code skill writes the Faust, measures the patch for the defects that do not show up by ear, a macro that only moves volume, a control that clips at the top of its own range, and builds the page. Two of the six still carry a measured defect, named on their card, because they double as the test cases that prove the measurement works.

__DEV__

A side project by one person, built first for its author. The source, the method and the measured negative results are on GitHub; where it is going is in VISION.md.

The instruments are Faust compiled to WebAssembly. Names are descriptions, not makes: no instrument here claims to be, or to be endorsed by, the machine it takes after.
